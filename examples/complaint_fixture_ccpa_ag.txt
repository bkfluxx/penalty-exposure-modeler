COMPLAINT FOR CIVIL PENALTIES AND INJUNCTIVE RELIEF

1. Plaintiff alleges that the defendant, operator of a consumer wellness
application with 3.4 million users, sold the personal information of
3,400,000 consumers to advertising partners without providing notice or an
opportunity to opt out, in violation of the California Consumer Privacy Act.

2. The defendant failed to process opt-out requests submitted through
user-enabled global privacy controls, and failed to cure the alleged violations
within 30 days of notice. Plaintiff seeks a civil penalty of up to $7,500 for
each intentional violation under Cal. Civ. Code 1798.155.

3. The defendant also collected face geometry templates from 900,000 consumers
residing in Texas for the purpose of emotion detection, without providing the
notice and obtaining the consent required by Texas law.

4. Employees of the defendant, acting on the defendant's behalf, shared
consumer health data with third parties in exchange for payment, without
obtaining valid authorisation from 210,000 consumers, in violation of RCW
19.373.

5. The defendant is a covered entity and failed to implement access controls
across 14,000 records, and did not correct the deficiency within 30 days of
discovering it, contrary to 45 CFR 164.312.

6. The defendant's subsidiary collected the personal information of
children under 13 in its mobile game without verifiable parental consent, and
failed to provide the notices required by the Children's Online Privacy
Protection Rule.

7. The defendant previously paid $375,000 to resolve allegations that it sold
personal information without notice, and $1.2 million in a related settlement.
